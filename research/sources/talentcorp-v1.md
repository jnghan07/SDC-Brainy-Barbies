###### Background

On 7 March 2024, Talent Corporation Malaysia 
Berhad (TalentCorp) was mandated to serve as the 
strategic think tank for Malaysia’s Ministry of Human 
Resources (KESUMA). The mandate aims to develop the 
government’s intelligence capacity to craft strategies 
for policy development, advocacy, and future planning. 
One of the first initiatives assigned to TalentCorp is to 
conduct a study on the impact of AI, Digital, and Green 
Economy on Malaysia’s workforce. The study will help 
guide policymakers and industries in preparing for future 
workforce demands.

###### Purpose of the Report

The study could not have come at a more appropriate 
time. The integration of AI, Digital, and Green Economy 
trends in Malaysia is recognised as a game changer, 
fundamentally altering the nation’s economic trajectory.

These trends are congruent with global movements 
towards technological innovation, environmental 
sustainability, and advanced economic growth.

Aligned with the aspirations of the MADANI Economy, 
Malaysia is undergoing a transformation from a labourintensive, commodity-based economy to one that is 
knowledge-centric and technologically sophisticated. 
National blueprints such as, but not limited to, the 
New Industrial Master Plan 2030 (NIMP 2030), Malaysia 
Digital Economy Blueprint (MDEB), and Twelfth Malaysia 
Plan (RMKe-12) highlight the strategic shift towards AI, 
Digital, and Green Economy.

These research studies cover several sectors, namely 
ICT; Food Manufacturing and Services; Pharmaceutical 
Manufacturing; Medical Devices; Aerospace; Electrical 
and Electronics; Wholesale and Retail Trade; Energy 
and Power; Chemical; and Global Business Services. 
These 10 key sectors have played a pivotal role in

driving Malaysia’s economic growth. Collectively, these 
sectors contribute approximately 60% of the country’s 
GDP, amounting to RM933 billion, and employ 31% of 
Malaysia’s skilled and semi-skilled workforce, which 
is equivalent to 3.5 million individuals. This substantial 
impact underscores the importance of these sectors 
to Malaysia’s overall economic performance. The study 
is ongoing across various sectors, not limited to the 10 
listed, with the objective of comprehensively covering all 
relevant sectors within Malaysia.

The outcomes of this study will contribute to the Malaysia 
National Skills Registry (MyNSR), a skills taxonomy 
that will be integrated into the MyMAHIR platform. This 
platform offers comprehensive insights into industry 
trends, job roles, required skills, career pathways, and 
available training programmes across all sectors.

###### Why AI, Digital, and Green Economy?

###### Malaysia’s Strategic AI Investment and Challenges in Readiness

• In tandem with the rise of AI, the Malaysian government 
launched the Malaysia AI Consortium and the AI Talent 
Roadmap for Malaysia 2024-2033 to prepare the 
workforce. YAB Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim 
emphasised the importance of mastering AI and digital 
transformation, stating, “If we do not master AI and 
digital transformation, we will lose out because in all 
areas of government management, defence, police, 
immigration, private sector, and agriculture, AI will 
dominate.”

• Malaysia’s AI readiness has been showing a declining 
trend. Between 2019 and 2023, its ranking in the Asia 
Pacific (APAC) region dropped from 5th to 8th, though 
it remains the 2nd ranked country in ASEAN. This trend 
mirrors the performance of other Southeast Asian 
countries, such as Indonesia, Thailand, the Philippines, 
and Vietnam, which all scored below the APAC average, 
highlighting a disparity between mature and emerging 
digital economies in the region.

###### The Evolving Digital Economy and Malaysia’s Competitiveness

• Digital economy is one of Malaysia’s key economic 
pillars, contributing 22.6% to Malaysia’s GDP, with 
projections to rise to 25.5% by 2025. The fast-evolving 
landscape of the digital economy necessitates a new 
strategy to remain relevant and resilient. To address 
this, Malaysia Digital, a national strategic initiative, 
was launched by the Malaysia Digital Economy 
Corporation (MDEC) in collaboration with the Ministry 
of Communications and Multimedia to drive adoption 
and opportunities in the digital economy.

• Malaysia’s digital competitiveness has experienced 
fluctuations. In the recent IMD World Digital 
Competitiveness Ranking, Malaysia’s position slipped 
from 26th in 2019 to 33rd in 2023. Despite being ranked 
2nd in ASEAN, countries like Indonesia and Thailand 
are catching up as they recorded improvements. This 
contrast highlights the need for Malaysia to bolster 
its efforts in enhancing digital competitiveness and 
maintaining its edge in the region.

###### Malaysia’s Green Economy Ambitions and Energy Transition Challenges

• Malaysia is also making significant strides in the Green 
Economy. The RMKe-12 outlines the nation’s aspiration 
to achieve net-zero greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions as 
early as 2050. Complementing this, the National Energy 
Policy (2022–2040) sets the foundation for transforming 
the energy landscape towards sustainability. In line with 
these objectives, the Malaysian government has also 
developed the National Energy Transition Roadmap 
(NETR) to accelerate the shift from a traditional fossil 
fuel-based economy to a high-value green economy.

• Malaysia’s efforts are reflected in its leading position 
in the World Economic Forum Energy Transition 
Index, where it ranked 1st in ASEAN and 35th globally. 
This is largely due to strong energy security driven by 
supply diversity and reliability. However, the country 
has demonstrated negative progress in three (3) out 
of five (5) sustainable dimension indicators over the 
past three (3) years, notably in Energy Intensity, CO2
per Capita, and Share of Electricity. This underscores 
the need for aligning to existing national strategies to 
ensure balanced and sustained progress in all aspects 
of the energy transition and sustainability goals for the 
country.

The emergence of these transformative trends will 
fundamentally change the nature of work in Malaysia. In 
anticipation of this shift, the government aims to enable 
our workforce through upskilling and reskilling initiatives 
to ensure Malaysian professionals possess the essential 
competencies in AI, Digital, and Green Economy, 
equipping them to excel in a dynamic, future-oriented 
economy.

### Research Methodology

The study focused on three (3) key trends shaping today’s workforce: **AI, Digital,** and **Green Economy.** Their definition 
is outlined below:

##### 01 Artificial Intelligence

Development and use of machine learning models capable of 
performing tasks that would have required human intelligence (deep 
learning, computer vision, Natural Language Processing (NLP), 
reinforcement learning, supervised and unsupervised learning).<sup>1</sup>

# Digital

# Green Economy

Activities and transactions driven by the public and various 
organisations to produce, adapt and innovate digital technologies and 
services for enhanced productivity and quality of life (big data analytics, 
cloud, Internet of Things (IoT), and robotic process automation).<sup>2</sup>

Employment growth and income driven by investment in low-carbon, 
resource-efficient, and socially inclusive economic activities, 
infrastructure, and assets.<sup>3</sup>

To effectively analyse how the key trends impact existing roles, four (4) key parameters have been defined in the 
assessment process, as stated below:

###### AI & Digital

1. **Opportunity to automate data-driven or low-creativity activities that are repetitive or rule-based via** 
**AI or other technology tools.**

2. **Human intervention is required despite some or most activities being automated or digitalised, as:**

• Strategic thinking and problem-solving are vital to making decisions

• Creative thinking is needed to generate new ideas or ways of working

• Outcomes need to be communicated or socialised and regulated

• High importance is placed on human emotions or physical involvement in performing the activity

• Typically performed by a critical role that holds accountability or a role requiring certification

###### Green Economy

1. **Impact of the environment on jobs that depend on limited natural resources and produce outputs that 
are polluting or may pollute the environment.**

2. **Opportunity to diversify, requiring new skills to implement the organisation’s Environment, Social, 
and Governance (ESG) agenda, which includes:**

• Environment: Areas for improvement in environmental sustainability

• Social: Diversity, equity, inclusivity, ethics, and community engagement

• Governance: Risk management, compliance, reporting, and corporate culture

• During the workshop for the **Electrical and Electronics** sector conducted as part of the study, large MNCs

###### Singapore: Financial incentives for MNCs - SMEs collaboration / incentives

The Partnerships for Capability Transformation (PACT) scheme promotes collaboration between 
multinational companies (MNCs) and local SMEs by offering financial support for investments and 
training, along with a programme manager to facilitate and de-risk these business partnerships.

For example, Google and Enterprise Singapore jointly introduced the Digital Practitioner Programme in 
May 2023 to help SMEs develop digital capabilities. Through a two (2) month training programme and a 
three (3) to six (6) month practicum with dedicated Google specialists, SMEs can enrol their employees 
in courses to develop skills in areas such as information technology support, project management, 
data analytics, user experience design, and digital marketing, helping them set up online stores and 
grow new sales channels.

As tech players roll out more AI-powered solutions targeting SMEs, the PACT programme, which 
leverages the network, expertise, and solutions of major tech companies to diffuse these technologies 
through workshops, on-site development, and training to develop operational use-cases, will 
accelerate the adoption of artificial intelligence among SMEs. This, in turn, will benefit the tech 
companies providing these solutions.

### Medium and Low Impacted Roles Analysis

As technology automates repetitive tasks, professionals are empowered to pursue new or higher-value activities, creating 
opportunities for medium impacted roles in AI, Digital, Green Economy. It has been identified that 90% of roles in the 
study are not highly impacted by AI, Digital, and Green Economy trends; among these, 62% are moderately impacted, 
while the remaining 28% experience low impact.

The impact of AI, Digital, and Green Economy trends on various sectors reveals that the majority of roles (90%) remain 
moderately or minimally impacted by these developments. This suggests that these advancements aim to enhance 
and complement the existing workforce as opposed to replacing it. However, specific sectors such as Aerospace and 
Electrical and Electronics exhibit high percentages of medium impacted roles – 89% and 88%, respectively. This indicates 
that while these roles remain relevant, they must evolve and upskill to keep pace with the automation and technological 
advancements transforming these sectors.

On the other hand, the ICT sector stands out, with most roles falling under the low impacted category. This is attributed 
to the sector’s widespread adoption of AI and digital initiatives, which has effectively mitigated high levels of job 
displacement.

###### Government

The government’s provision of funding and providing incentives to support the adoption of new technologies, upskill 
and reskill the workforce, and leverage existing infrastructure as training centres promises a more competitive and 
technologically advanced Malaysia.

Financial constraints are the primary challenge for companies adopting new technologies, limiting their ability to remain 
competitive. Additionally, the rapid pace of technological advancement requires a workforce equipped with specialised 
skills and a deep understanding of modern technical systems. Compounding this issue, Malaysia faces a talent drain, 
as many highly skilled professionals seek opportunities abroad, creating a gap in the local expertise critical for national 
development.

To address these challenges, three (3) financial-related initiatives are being proposed for the government’s consideration. 
Industry players believe that government intervention is essential to strategically support businesses navigating economic 
advancement challenges.

###### 01 Financial Incentives for R&D Efforts

Provide financial incentives, including tax deductions and loans, for companies to invest in R&D efforts to adopt AI, 
Digital, and Green Economy technologies.

Offer **low-interest loans** or loan guarantees to support capital investments, such as the purchase of new 
equipment for facility upgrades

In addition to supporting R&D efforts, funds should also be allocated to companies to **secure intellectual 
property ownership** derived from their R&D results

Introduce a range of financial incentives such **as tax breaks, tax credits, and deductions for R&D expenditures,** 
investments in sustainable technology, or the adoption of Industry 4.0 technologies

Provide **subsidies to upgrade IT infrastructure,** ensuring it can effectively support future technological 
advancements and green practices

###### 02 Financial Support to Upskill and Reskill Employees

Provide financial support and grants for companies to upskill and reskill their employees in AI, Digital, and Green Economy 
skills, as well as other sector-specific skills.

Establish a **co-funding model** to encourage greater public-private investment in upskilling and reskilling 
programmes, lowering the barriers for companies to invest in workforce development

Subsidise the cost of training and provide **financial aid options for individuals** who demonstrate potential or 
need

Offer **tax relief** to companies that invest in their employees’ training and development programmes on AI, Digital, 
and Green Economy

Review the **TVET programme** as a place for impacted roles for upskilling

staff turnover.

Furthermore, a noticeable gap exists between the skills the workforce possesses and those imparted by educational 
and training institutions. This discrepancy poses a significant challenge in effectively bridging skill gaps. Nevertheless, 
industry players are cognisant of their pivotal role in shaping the talent landscape and driving progress.

###### Academia

The current education curriculum needs to be redesigned to better equip students with the skills and knowledge required 
for the real world, particularly by enhancing internship programmes. It is important to acknowledge the existing gap 
between the skills possessed by graduates and the demands of employers, leading to a significant disparity in the 
workforce.

The emergence of new job roles necessitates competencies that are not currently prioritised in education. Furthermore, 
the changing nature of industries means students need greater exposure to real workplace practices and culture, making 
the shift from academic to professional environments quite challenging. It is increasingly evident that educational 
programmes must adapt alongside industry advancements to ensure that graduates are adequately prepared for the 
workforce.

###### 07 Refresh Education Curriculum (Starting from Pre-School)

Realign education curriculum according to sector demands and adapt to address evolving workforce needs.

Involve industry experts in the curriculum development process to ensure that the **content is practical, up-todate, and relevant,** particularly courses related to automation, digital, and sustainability

Introduce **students to AI and digital-related subjects from a young age** and maintain a dynamic curriculum 
that can quickly adapt to technological advancements

Establishing advisory boards/ channels for **regular feedback on curriculum** relevance and teaching 
effectiveness can help educators fine-tune their approaches to meet industry standards

###### 08 Extend Internship Programmes

Increase the frequency of internships to keep up with sector trends and provide students with valuable hands-on 
experience and practical problem-solving opportunities.

Internships with **longer tenure (6-12 months)** with industry players provide students with opportunities to gain 
invaluable industry experience, build professional networks, and understand day-to-day operations

Develop internship and apprenticeship programmes **that integrate industry-driven projects** into the syllabus 
to give students practical problem-solving opportunities

Make **practical attachments** a compulsory component of training programmes; involve on-the-job training, 
internships, or real-world projects for participants to apply their knowledge, reinforce continuous learning, and 
improve skill retention

###### Training Providers

In professional training, there is a pressing need to improve the quality of content delivered by training providers. This 
can be achieved by bringing more certified and reputable trainers on board to ensure that the training material meets the 
specific requirements of different sectors. The professional training landscape is riddled with challenges. Outdated and 
generic content often fails to meet the unique needs of various sectors. Additionally, there is a need for more credible 
training providers and regular assessments to ensure that training remains relevant.

Training programmes must be more effective, impart enduring skills, and align with the industry’s operational needs. 
Moreover, the emphasis on soft skills neglects the need for sector-specific expertise, which results in a gap in developing 
specialised skills for industry professionals. Industry stakeholders emphasise the pivotal role of training providers in 
addressing the talent landscape needs and bridging skill gaps.

###### Develop Micro-Credentials Training Programmes 09

Develop micro-credentials training programmes to focus on AI, Digital, and Green Economy topics that align with sector 
needs.

Develop training programmes that are strategically aligned with **recognised industry certifications,** ensuring 
trainees gain credentials to enhance their career progression opportunities

Create **short, focused modules** that can be stacked towards a more prominent credential and provide microcredentials that recognise specific skills and competencies

Provide a range of upskilling training programmes that cater to various roles within the sector by **offering 
accredited certification courses** specifically designed for the sector, focusing on foundational and advanced 
concepts

###### Onboard Certified Trainers 10

Onboard certified trainers that tailor the delivery of training programmes to various learning styles and ensure content 
remains relevant to real-world applications.

Offer **various delivery methods,** including online, in-person, and hybrid models, to accommodate different 
learning preferences and schedules by utilising microlearning and modular approaches

Assess training providers’ **credibility and certification status** to ensure they meet the specific needs and sector 
standards

Invest in comprehensive **train-the-trainer programmes** to ensure trainers are well-equipped to teach new 
technologies and methodologies

Implement a **quality assurance programme** that regularly evaluates the effectiveness of training delivery and 
content

###### Skilling Up Ireland for an AI future

In October 2023, the Ireland Ministry of State for Trade Promotion, Digital, and Company Regulation, in 
collaboration with Microsoft, launched a national AI skilling programme to provide people of all ages, 
experiences, and backgrounds with the opportunity to upskill for an AI future.

###### Initiatives:

• Microsoft Ireland’s education team has created a tailored 6-week **‘Introduction to AI’** course for individuals 
with minimal or no AI knowledge. Designed for anyone over 18, the course aims to provide foundational AI 
knowledge and skills through live virtual sessions.<sup>45</sup> Upon completion, participants will receive a digital 
badge and have the opportunity to take an exam to earn a Professional Certificate, all at no cost.

• The **‘Skills for Jobs’** initiative offers courses to help graduates and workers upgrade their Digital and AI 
skills, providing access to eight (8) Professional Certificates for in-demand tech jobs. It includes an AI 
pathway for a Professional Certificate in Gen-AI, delivered in partnership with Fastrack into Information 
Technology.

###### Testimonials from small business owners from Ireland on its proven benefits for SMEs:<sup>46</sup>

• **Andrew Murray, General Manager of the Doonmore Hotel in Inishbofin,** sees AI as a tool to save 
him time on everything from scheduling staff to inventory and invoicing. After learning AI fundamentals 
and tools like Microsoft Copilot in a course, Murray shared his enthusiasm for AI’s time-saving 
capabilities, stating, *“What I am interested in the most is the power of AI to save time for people like me 
because time is the most precious thing we have.”* He plans to apply AI to streamline administrative 
tasks, noting that AI can accomplish in minutes what would take him a week.

• **Catherine O’Connor works on one of her heddle looms.** She is a weaver who draws on the colours 
and textures of Inishbofin in her work. She markets her artwork using Copilot. She said that in the AI 
training, she learned how to put in a detailed description of a particular work and the idea behind it to 
create a prompt for Copilot. Copilot then produced a few sentences that made a good starting point 
for a post or website description. *“Before I would be sitting there a long time, or I would ask my sister 
to come up with some words -she was always telling me I needed to fluff it up a bit because I’m trying 
to sell something,” O’Connor said. “It is only when I did the course that I saw how easy it was to do it.”*

The ‘Skill Up Ireland’; initiative presents a replicable model for Malaysia, offering the potential to significantly enhance the 
digital competencies of the local workforce and drive innovation, especially for SMEs.

### Conclusion

The advancements in AI, Digital, and Green Economy 
are making waves and will demand significant workforce 
changes over the next three (3) to five (5) years. This 
impact study has revealed the implications for the 
workforce across 10 sectors due to the continued and 
rapid progress in these areas. A total of **949 roles were 
validated,** comprising **889 existing roles crucial for 
maintaining industry standards** and operational 
efficiency, and **60 emerging roles** highlighted due to 
their increasing relevance with the rise of these trends.

These 949 roles have been categorised into highly 
impacted, medium impacted, low impacted, and 
emerging roles. **Highly impacted** roles are those at risk 
of convergence or displacement, requiring workers to 
pivot to adjacent roles and reskill. **Medium impacted** 
roles remain relevant but need to evolve and upskill to 
meet new expectations. **Low impacted** roles are not 
severely affected but require ongoing self-improvement 
to stay relevant. **Emerging roles are** set to drive 
future advancements and innovations across the 10 
sectors, playing a vital role in navigating the changes 
brought about by AI, Digital, and Green Economy. The 
study provided valuable insights into the demand for 
these roles, guiding both employees and employers in 
planning their career and talent development strategies. 
Additionally, it highlights the need for holistic, crosscutting, and targeted interventions to ensure that 
workers are adequately prepared to meet the industry’s 
evolving talent needs.

The stakeholders have recommended 10 common 
initiatives across 10 sectors to implement in the talent

ecosystem. Government, Industry Players, Academia, 
and Training Providers each have important roles to 
play in ensuring the necessary reforms, transitions, and 
interventions are in place to prepare the workforce for a 
future-ready sector and contribute to Malaysia’s longterm socioeconomic goals.

Furthermore, **the impact study also proposed three 
(3) Whole-of-Nation Enablers, which will be vital 
to expediting the transformation of the Malaysian 
workforce.** The success factors for the whole-ofnation implementation approach include; ensuring 
clear accountability and responsibilities for publicprivate stakeholders; maintaining transparency through 
periodic progress reporting; establishing effective 
linkages with multiple stakeholders through consistent 
and open communication; and executing with discipline 
to meet the defined milestones. These factors are 
essential for the success of the implementation and 
governance of proposed initiatives.

Embarking on this journey of continuous adaptation 
and innovation will be vital in preparing the workforce 
for ongoing advancements in AI, Digital, and Green 
Economy. To achieve this, the MyMAHIR Future 
Skills Talent Council (FSTC) will conduct regular 
needs assessments to identify immediate and future 
workforce skills gaps, analyse talent demands by 
sector and educational level, propose strategies, 
determine essential sector-specific skills, and 
periodically update these skills in response to 
technological advancements and evolving operating 
environments.
